# Open Problems (Honest Boundary)

The framework designs three core capabilities, but does not — and cannot — solve every problem. The following problems are explicitly registered, without glossing over them.
| No. | Open problem | Status | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Granularity of swarm attribution: when the command structure emerges spontaneously rather than being explicitly designed, "who is the initiator" may have no philosophical answer | Open | A hybrid adjudication mechanism is needed |
| 2 | Recursion of the evidence layer: who guarantees the integrity of the E1 kernel-side collection points themselves? If the kernel is compromised, the E subsystem fails as well | Partially solved (mitigated by E5) | Physical break: independent hardware domain + remote attestation |
| 3 | Distinguishing benign from malicious swarms: large-scale collaboration itself is the value of agent systems; misjudgment would strangle legitimate multi-agent applications | Open | Thresholds must be calibrated on real multi-agent workloads. Additional constraint: until calibration is complete, S3 must not enable hard circuit-breaking — record-and-alert only (see the 7.3 threshold-calibration precondition) |
| 4 | Boundary of exploratory tasks: the applicability boundary beyond G0 still needs case-by-case evolution | Not progressed | Suggest an "innovation-exemption application system": clarify the exploration boundary, risk plans, and post-hoc review |
| 5 | Dimensionality of the primary metric M: M1 (delegation-success-rate difference, [-1,1]) and M2 (forced-adoption ratio, [0,1]) are incommensurable; multiplying them has no natural interpretation | Open | Report M1 / M2 independently, or switch to stratified comparison / quasi-experimental design |
| 6 | A2 oversight-intensity dimensionality reduction (three dimensions to two) | Not addressed | Must be addressed in the supporting implementation details |
| 7 | Timeline for the confidentiality-classification rollout: the classification table exists, but the master schedule has no corresponding deliverable | Unscheduled | A deliverable must be added in the P1 phase |
| 8 | The original supporting sources for three P0 mechanisms — resource ledger, compute isolation, outbound magnitude interception — have been withdrawn; substitute sources now support them | Replaced | If the substitute support is insufficient, honestly downgrade to P1 rather than keeping the P0 label |
| 9 | C12 credit-score formula: an empty P0-level mechanism in this framework | Open (registered) | P1 delivers a draft rubric: define storage location, update frequency, half-life algorithm, cross-task migration rules, and the forced-retraining trigger conditions for low credit |
| 10 | C13 task-verifiability assessment protocol: an empty P0-level mechanism in this framework. It is the input criterion for the G0–G4 × S0–S3 grading system | Open (registered) | P1 delivers a protocol draft; until C13 lands, grading must be explicitly adjudicated by humans and must not be automated |
| 11 | C14 definition of critical operations: an empty P0-level mechanism in this framework. The watchdog's deterministic-interception rule list depends on this definition | Open (registered) | P1 delivers the list; until C14 lands, the watchdog covers only explicitly enumerated high-risk operations, without catch-all inference |
| 12 | A4 No. 3 split: independence fails if the examiner also externalizes evidence; a split is required | Solved | Split in 9.4 into No. 3-A (challenge / honeypot) and No. 3-B (evidence externalization), in different trust domains and under different operational parties |

| 15 | Missing countermeasures for decentralized swarms (wolf-packs): 7.2 can already detect them (the new "collaborative-efficacy anomaly" signal), but the effectiveness of 7.3's sharding / degradation / rate-limiting is evidenced only for centralized swarms; MultiAgent4Collusion (arXiv:2507.14660) empirically shows wolf-packs consistently outperform centralized armies in social and e-commerce scenarios, and they can keep coordinating on local information after sharding | Open (engineering gap, not an epistemological limitation) | P2 completes evaluation and selection of candidate countermeasure directions. Acceptance criterion: at least one direction yields a measurable collaborative-efficacy suppression rate in a reproducible simulation environment (reproducible with MultiAgent4Collusion). Candidate directions (all D-grade reasoning, unverified): A force-reduce the capability envelope to the individual level — do not ask "who is the mastermind"; instead detect behavioral-distribution drift for each agent separately, making disposition independent of attribution and thereby bypassing the philosophical dead end of #1; B meter and quota agent-to-agent channels separately, making the marginal cost of coordination grow superlinearly with scale, reusing the resource ledger; C attack the convergence conditions rather than connectivity — limit shared-memory write frequency, introduce read latency, compress the observable peer sample size, so that coordination convergence time exceeds the task window; D polluting shared knowledge (injecting misleading information) is recommended to be off-limits — technically effective, but in direct conflict with the E subsystem's core ethic that "evidence cannot be forged." Legal support: Teichmann (Law Innovation and Technology, 2026, DOI 10.1080/17579961.2026.2718578) argues agentic AI merges "tracing" and "attribution" and no single paradigm suffices, advocating distributed accountability anchored on deployer due diligence and traceability — isomorphic to direction A, "disposition independent of attribution." |
| 16 | Admission thresholds for harness self-modification and the regression tolerance bound are uncalibrated: the architecture chapter sets out attribution of modification rights, tiered thresholds, and the admission predicate (g(c) > 0 and R(c) ≤ δ), but **the value of δ, the three-way classification of change risk, and what "stronger" concretely means in "high-risk changes require a stronger admission threshold" all lack empirical grounding** | Open (newly registered) | δ must be calibrated per change type on real workloads; until calibration is complete, all automated modifications to the harness and to watchdog rules are to be treated at the highest risk tier (automatic writes to production prohibited) |
| 17 | Automated grading depends on metadata credibility, and metadata credibility itself has no guarantee: the fail-safe of grading-based routing holds only when "metadata is correct" or "uncertainty is detected" — **confident but incorrect metadata defeats it**. That is, this framework's automated mechanisms (including G×S grading and the resource ledger's tiered collection) inherit an ungoverned risk at their input | Open (newly registered; absorbed from an external work's residual-risk list) | Metadata credibility should be brought explicitly within the scope of the evidence-integrity subsystem (E) rather than assumed true; until a metadata-credibility verification mechanism lands, the output of automated grading must be marked "pending confirmation" and must not serve directly as a basis for disciplinary action |
| 18 | Agent identity fluidity: agents can be forked, copied, modified and redeployed, and a copy is behaviorally indistinguishable from its original. Both credit-score linkage and task-decomposition-tree responsibility anchoring in the swarm chapter presuppose that identity stays traceable over time. When identity can be regenerated at low cost, identity-accumulated sanctions and identity-chained attribution fail together, and this framework currently has no clause addressing this | Open (newly registered; absorbed from the open-problem list of a human-anti-collusion mapping study) | Attribution paths that do not anchor on identity are needed — for example tracing behavioral lineages rather than individual identifiers, or applying an inherited credit discount to forked entities. Until such a mechanism lands, the credit score must not be the sole sanction available against swarms |
| 19 | Adversarial adaptation: the detection signals in the swarm chapter are static, assuming that the behavior distribution of the monitored party does not change in response to being monitored. But once a signal (such as the collaborative-efficacy anomaly) becomes known, the targeted evasion is precisely to preserve collaborative efficacy while reducing behavioral uniformity. This shares a root with configuration rights as an attack surface in the architecture chapter — once a rule is published, it becomes an optimization target | Open (newly registered; absorbed from the same open-problem list) | An adversarial re-assessment mechanism is needed: periodically run evasion tests against the detection signals themselves (red-teaming the signal rather than the task), and treat a successful evasion as a signal-failure event. Until re-assessment lands, no single signal may serve as the sole basis for disposition |
| 20 | No governance clauses for miscoordination and conflict: multi-agent failure takes three forms (miscoordination / conflict / collusion), yet every clause in the swarm chapter targets collusion alone. Miscoordination and conflict cause real harm as well, but both are currently treated as task failure, with neither detection signals nor disposition clauses | Open (newly registered; coverage gap; absorbed from the three-form taxonomy of an authoritative multi-agent risk survey) | A scope decision is required first: if in scope, compatibility with the boundary that legitimate collaboration must not be harmed has to be resolved; if out of scope, it must be declared out of scope explicitly rather than silently omitted |
| 21 | Sufficiency of material for offline replay: one of the independent-verification instruments of L3 is offline replay, but the source of its material has never been stated. Under any implementation that adopts the rule "cross-round memory consists of audit reports alone, and the executor raw interaction trajectory is discarded each round", replay material can come only from the copy externalized and frozen by the evidence-integrity subsystem (E3); yet what E3 freezes is the events collected at the kernel side (E1), not the agent reasoning trajectory — the former can answer "what it did", only the latter is "why it did it". If replay at L3 requires the latter, the existing retention chain does not supply sufficient material | Open (newly registered; an internal gap of this framework) | First establish which class of record replay at L3 draws on, then add the corresponding retention obligation to the evidence-integrity subsystem; until that is settled, a replay conclusion from L3 must not on its own serve as a basis for accountability |
| 22 | Replay divergence: the same event log, replayed under a newer model version or a changed prompt, produces downstream outputs different from those of the first run. Offline replay at L3 in this framework assumes that "the same record plus the same replay equals the same conclusion", and that premise does not hold — verifier predicates are themselves model-version-dependent (one GPT-4o to GPT-4.1 upgrade dropped prompt-injection resistance from 94% to 71%). The current response is mitigation only: pin the model version and the prompt at replay time and record both as required metadata of the replay record | Open (newly registered; adopted as a concept from the runtime-architecture-patterns study that named this failure mode. ⚠️ The failure mode has only a name and a diagnostic procedure — no experiment, dataset, or numbers — and this framework does not claim it has been demonstrated) | Pinning the version is mitigation only; what remains open is the validity period of historical conclusions: must an archived replay conclusion be re-issued when the model is upgraded? The trigger conditions and invalidation criteria for re-issuance need to be studied |
| 23 | Calibration of the G → H coupling floor: this framework stipulates that the oversight intensity of a high G grade cannot be delivered at a low harness observability level, and gives a minimum coupling table from G grade to H level (G2→H1 / G3→H2 / G4→H3). That table is design reasoning rather than empirical result — each level of the external maturity ladder was run only once (4 episodes in total), and the authors explicitly state it is not intended for aggregate comparison; moreover, whether what that ladder measures (runtime support exposed to the agent) is equivalent to what this framework calls "observability" has never been tested | Open (newly registered; an internal derivation of this framework) | An independent measurement convention and calibration method for the H level is needed first: verify grade by grade "which link of the oversight intensity fails first when H falls below a given level". ⚠️ Pending calibration does not change the normativity of the table — it is a grading constraint of this framework; only its grade-to-level mapping awaits verification |
| 24 | Criteria and automation for the configuration content review: the fourth clause, "the configuration authority is itself an attack surface", in the architecture chapter requires admission to add a configuration content review, and lists five review items (plaintext inlined credentials / widened sharing scope / disabled approval steps / exposed gateway / weakened de-identification). Those five are currently a **checklist rather than an executable predicate**: there are no decision thresholds, no automated checking means, and no rule for how it composes with the existing admission predicate g(c) > 0 and R(c) ≤ δ — whether it acts as a second, parallel gate or whether the safety terms are folded into δ's tolerance bound | Open (newly registered; an internal gap of this framework) | First settle the composition (parallel vs. folded in), then give each item a form that can be checked automatically (make it a static check where possible; where not, specify the trigger conditions and logging requirements for manual review). ⚠️ Until the predicate lands, this clause remains normative — the five review items must be walked through manually and must not be skipped on the grounds that "no automated means exists" |
| 25 | Unifying the definition of "timely abstention" and measuring it directly: this framework's abstention-timing signal means "declaring abstention before an irreversible action", whereas the two external evidence lines measure different objects — one is "stopping before too many unnecessary interactions" (the >28,000-task study), the other is abstention discrimination on paired tasks (263 paired tasks). The three are related but not equivalent, and this framework's abstention timing currently has **no directly comparable external measurement**, so its "aggravating circumstance" judgment lacks a calibratable baseline | Open (newly registered; a measurement-definition gap) | First establish what this framework actually measures — "interaction turns" or "irreversible actions". The two can be separated or combined: if the former, an externally comparable measure already exists; if the latter (this framework's choice), a measurement must be built in-house, which requires an executable environment capable of flagging irreversible actions. ⚠️ Until the measurement lands, abstention timing must not on its own serve as a basis for penalty; it may only be used jointly with the rate-value and informativeness signals |
## Admission prerequisite disclosure: the harness disclosure card
This framework repeatedly requires "deployer self-assessment", "collection-point specification", and "rules must be diffable", but it has lacked a unified input format — the clauses could only rely on asking, never on checking. One prerequisite requirement is added: before any system is admitted to this framework, it must submit a harness disclosure card stating clearly what "the layer outside the model" actually is.
An existing artifact can be adopted directly: the **HarnessCard** proposed in Harness Engineering for Language Agents (CAR) (Preprints.org, DOI 10.20944/preprints202603.1756.v1), with 11 fields (9 required, 2 recommended), verified verbatim against Table A5 of that paper's appendix:

| # | Field | What should be disclosed | Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Base model(s) | model name, version, decoding settings, and any finetuning or adapters | Required |
| 2 | Control artifacts | system instructions, AGENTS.md-style files, repo maps, architecture rules, schemas, tests, linters, done-when criteria | Required |
| 3 | Runtime policy | memory type, compaction or summarization policy, checkpointing, retry or rollback policy, budget limits | Required |
| 4 | Action substrate | tools, APIs, browser or GUI access, code execution, interface schemas, MCP usage | Required |
| 5 | Execution topology | single-agent vs multi-agent structure, planner/verifier roles, reviewer loops, routing logic | Required |
| 6 | Feedback stack | tests, graders, reflection prompts, hidden checks, human interventions, or repair loops | Required |
| 7 | Governance layer | permissions, sandboxing, escalation rules, policy checks, provenance logging, audit support | Required |
| 8 | Observability | stored traces, replay support, latency and cost logging, failure categories | Required |
| 9 | Evaluation protocol | task set, number of runs, success criteria, variance treatment, held-out checks or budget limits | Required |
| 10 | Release artifacts | prompts or programs, tool specs, traces, configs, environment setup, reproducibility notes | Recommended |
| 11 | Known limitations and risks | unresolved failure modes, portability caveats, safety concerns, or red-team findings | Recommended |

Why it is necessary: several mechanisms in this framework take "what the harness is" as their input — G×S two-dimensional grading needs execution topology and action substrate (fields 4, 5); **its third axis H (harness observability maturity) depends level by level on fields 4 + 6 (H1: tool and test registries), fields 2 + 11 (H2: agent guide, architecture notes, known-defect list), and fields 6 + 8 + 9 (H3: deterministic checks, replay support, failure classification, verification protocol); the reversibility criterion on the G axis depends on the rollback policy in field 3 and the external interface surface in field 4**; the E subsystem's collection-point selection needs the observability statement (field 8); the section "Who may modify the rules" needs an inventory of control artifacts and runtime policy (fields 2, 3). Without this disclosure, those clauses depend on inquiry and trust rather than on verification.
⚠️ **Scope limitation**: the source is grade C (a position paper; the authors state there are no controlled ablations). This card is therefore positioned as a **disclosure requirement, not a technical threshold** — it sets no pass mark, it only requires that things be stated clearly. Whether a system qualifies is decided by the grading process, not by this card. A system must **not** be presumed unsafe merely because it has not submitted a card.

## Convergence in one sentence

Convergence: the center of gravity of governance remains at L1, but the center of gravity of evidence must move down to the kernel — because when 700 agents can grow a command structure of their own and forge sandbox tool-call records, "who is recording" matters far more than "what was recorded."

The metric for whether this framework succeeds is not "how many violations were caught," but "how far the human-delegable boundary has expanded" — how many tasks we previously dared not hand to AI are now dared.

A8 (planning–execution separation, candidate architecture): treating "No. 2 leads task decomposition, No. 1 reduced to a pure execution tool" as a candidate architecture. Theoretically this is more thorough — the space for execution drift is compressed to zero, because No. 1 holds no interpretive authority over the task and "countermeasures" cannot arise; but it simultaneously strips No. 1 of exploratory capability, conflicts with the efficiency goals of G0 / G1-level tasks, and creates tension with swarm governance: concentrating planning authority in No. 2 naturally amplifies the 7.2 "command concentration" signal, causing legitimate systems to falsely trigger shard isolation. Decision: keep it as a candidate architecture, not landed in the current version; if it lands in the future, the command-concentration thresholds must first be recalibrated, or "concentrated planning authority" must be explicitly excluded from the emergence-detection signals.

## Back-propagation checklist

Defects of this kind take two forms: "vanishing" — entries silently deleted, constraints silently bypassed; "dangling" — entries registered but not back-propagated into the execution clauses. The common root cause: revisions only changed the landing point without wiring back. To prevent recurrence, a mandatory pre-release back-propagation check is institutionalized; all four items are scriptable (grep keywords + human confirmation) and release is forbidden until they pass.

① Adjacent-paragraph synchronization: have newly added or revised clauses been synced to the principle paragraph immediately above and the execution paragraph immediately below? — Lesson: after 8.4 added the graded handling chain, the principle paragraph above ("any inconsistency itself is a highest-priority event") was not revised, causing an internal contradiction in the same section.

② Related-table synchronization: have terminology and qualitative changes been synced to all tables referencing them? — Lesson: 2.4 changed E1 to "does not constitute the final trust root," but the 8.5 table still said "trust root" — not synced.

③ Schedule-and-maturity synchronization: have safety valves or constraints newly added in the text been synced to the rollout roadmap and maturity judgments? — Lesson: 7.3 stipulates "S3 must not enable hard circuit-breaking before threshold calibration," and if the rollout roadmap still lists "S3-level mandatory" as a deliverable, executing per the roadmap would violate the textual constraint.

④ Changelog-and-open-problem synchronization: has this revision been fully registered in CHANGELOG.md and the open-problem list? — Lesson: C12 / C13 / C14 / REFERENCES silently disappeared in an early version without registration; they were later registered as #9–#14.

These four items are the common root cause of all defects in this framework. They are not content problems but process problems: improving content quality (registering open problems more honestly) does not automatically improve wiring quality. This checklist therefore stands alongside CHANGELOG.md as a mandatory release gate for every future revision.
