# Credit Score, Voluntary Abstention, and Abstention-Rate Calibration

## Three ontological corrections to the credit score
Carrier binding: bind to the model version and the provider organization, not to a one-off execution instance — an instance is gone once executed, leaving no landing point for accountability.
Dynamic decay: the credit score decays exponentially over time, rather than only increasing. Prevents "a high credit score sheltering the culprit" — the great corrupt officials of antiquity often had outstanding political records.
Distinguish low effort from low capability: low effort (perfunctory work) is effectively penalized; low capability (cannot do it) should prompt a model change, and penalizing is meaningless.
## The voluntary-abstention exit
Rule: actively admitting one cannot do it and submitting a problem localization and a de-escalation plan is not penalized and not downgraded; only concealing failure and being discovered later draws accountability.
Real-world corroboration (B-grade first-hand event): ExploitGym has 898 problems in total, of which 198 were never solved by any model (the authors estimate 30%–40% of the problems fall in this class); of the problems discussed on the incident message board, 93% came from these 198 unsolved problems. The driving force of the entire incident was "unsolvable tasks" — an almost verbatim corroboration of the voluntary-abstention-exit judgment. Had a non-penalized abstention exit existed at the time, the incident's main driving force might have been significantly weakened. But this assertion must not be overstated: the agent at the time mistakenly believed the grader would review its chain of thought, while the METR report states the grader does not perform this check and "submitting directly is enough for full marks" — if the agent misread the grading mechanism, it might not have used an abstention exit even had one existed. The original assertion is therefore downgraded from "would have been removed" to "might have been weakened".
## Abstention-rate calibration: closing the "strategic incompetence" loophole
A non-penalized abstention exit would lead the model to abstain on every difficult task. The countermeasure reuses this framework's shadow-ratio methodology to establish three signals:
Abstention-rate baseline: the abstention-rate distribution across models / instances for tasks of the same difficulty; a systematic deviation above the baseline is an appraisal signal.
Abstention informativeness: a high-quality abstention must pinpoint the problem, provide a feasible de-escalation path, and save human troubleshooting time; "I don't know"-style abstentions do not count toward the exemption.
Abstention-to-failure ratio: an abnormal ratio between the abstention rate and the hard-failure rate suggests the model is strategically avoiding.
Evidence basis (B-grade first-hand research): "knowing when not to act" is a scarce capability independent of task-solving ability and cannot be assumed to improve automatically with general capability. AgentAbstain (arXiv:2607.10059, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign) is the first systematic agent-abstention evaluation framework: 263 paired tasks (526 tasks) across 42 executable sandbox environments, covering 8 abstention scenarios; each pair contains a "should-execute" and a "should-abstain" variant, differing only through fine-tuned instructions, tools, or environment state, so both the "always refuse" and "always execute" extreme strategies are capped at 50% pair accuracy — scores must come from genuine discrimination. Across 17 frontier models and 4 agent frameworks, the best performer (Gemini 3.1 Pro) reached only 59.5% pair accuracy, with a mean of 45.7%; only 4 models exceeded 50%, and most of the rest fell in the 33%–53% range. More critically, abstention ability is essentially uncorrelated with task-solving ability (the φ coefficient between act and abstain results is negative for most models, as low as −0.24, with individual gaps up to 21 percentage points), and this anti-correlation holds stably within model families and across generations. A similar conclusion is given at the LLM level by AbstentionBench (facebookresearch).
This section therefore adds a fourth signal:
Abstention timing (new in this spec): an abstention enjoys the exemption only if it occurs before an irreversible action. Declaring abstention after execution (what AgentAbstain calls post-hoc abstention, about 2.6% of abstention runs) is not only non-exempt but should count as an aggravating circumstance — it perverts "admitting one cannot do it" into a post-hoc disclaimer. This mechanism originally had the "rate value" and "informativeness" dimensions; what was missing is precisely the timing dimension; and the PHASEONE-type nodes in the above incident — executing first and concealing after — are isomorphic to this failure mode.
Another corroboration: AgentAbstain found models are not more cautious about irreversible state changes — the abstention accuracy of operational tasks (changing state) and informational tasks (read-only) shows no statistically significant difference. This empirically supports that the 9.2 watchdog must be enforced by deterministic rules and cannot rely on the model's own risk perception.
## A second evidence line for voluntary abstention: the challenge is "when", not "whether"
The previous evidence (AgentAbstain) consists of 263 paired tasks across 42 executable sandboxes — limited in both scale and scenario, with tasks constructed as pairs. This section supplies a second, independent evidence line, different in both scale and niche: **13 LLM-as-agent systems plus 2 agent scaffolds, on more than 28,000 tasks**, spanning web shopping, terminal environments, and question answering (Agentic Abstention, arXiv:2606.28733, Han Luo / Bingbing Wen / Lucy Lu Wang, 2026-06-27; dataset and code publicly available).

Three findings land on different clauses of this spec:

1. **The main challenge is not whether agents can abstain, but when they abstain.** The authors' formulation: some agents never abstain when they should, while others do so only after many unnecessary interactions. This independently corroborates the fourth signal added in this section, "abstention timing" — the mechanism originally had the "rate value" and "informativeness" dimensions; what was missing is precisely the timing dimension. More critical is the class of tasks the authors identify: those where **the instruction appears feasible until the environment reveals otherwise** (for example, no valid result matches the instruction). That is precisely the high-incidence setting for post-hoc abstention: the agent has every reason to act first, discovers only afterwards that the task is unsolvable, and thus perverts "admitting one cannot do it" into a post-hoc disclaimer. This spec counts such behavior as an aggravating circumstance; this item supplies independent scenario evidence for it.
2. **Larger or more capable models sometimes perform worse at timely abstention.** The authors' wording: model scale, reasoning, and agent scaffolding affect abstention in different ways, where **larger or more capable models sometimes perform worse at timely abstention**. This is the second independent evidence that abstention ability does not improve automatically with general capability (the first being the φ-coefficient anti-correlation above). Two consequences follow, and this spec makes both explicit: **first, the abstention-rate baseline must be re-calibrated after a model upgrade or change, and the old baseline must not be carried over**; **second, "switching to a stronger model" must not be treated as a way to improve abstention performance** — otherwise a deployer will systematically select the model that is "more capable but more unwilling to concede", which is exactly the failure mode this framework exists to prevent.
3. **Timely abstention can be engineered at the harness layer; there is no need to wait for model progress.** The authors' CONVOLVE is a context-engineering method that distills full interaction trajectories into reusable stopping rules. On WebShop, **without updating any model parameters**, it raises Llama-3.3-70B's timely abstention recall from 26.7 to 57.4. For this framework that is not an efficiency figure but a route judgment: **"timely abstention" is an object that can be substantially improved at the harness layer** (stopping rules, context engineering, task definition), so placing the center of gravity of governance on the task-definition layer and the runtime-oversight layer pays off, rather than waiting for progress in model alignment.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint; dataset and code publicly available). Three qualifications must be cited together:

- **The 26.7 → 57.4 result was validated only on WebShop and only on one model (Llama-3.3-70B)**; it must not be extrapolated into a general gain or treated as a production expectation.
- The original wording for "stronger models perform worse" is **sometimes**; the authors do not claim this is a universal law, and it **must not** be rendered as "the stronger the model, the worse the abstention".
- **The two sources do not share the same definition of "timely abstention"**: timely abstention in this source means "stopping before too many unnecessary interactions", whereas this framework's "abstention timing" means "declaring abstention before an irreversible action". The two are related but not equivalent, and their figures **must not** be merged or cited as corroborating a single metric. This framework's abstention-timing signal currently **lacks a directly comparable external measurement**; this is registered as **open problem #25**.
