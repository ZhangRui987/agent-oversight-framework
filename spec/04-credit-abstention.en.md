# Credit Score, Voluntary Abstention, and Abstention-Rate Calibration

## Three ontological corrections to the credit score
Carrier binding: bind to the model version and the provider organization, not to a one-off execution instance — an instance is gone once executed, leaving no landing point for accountability.
Dynamic decay: the credit score decays exponentially over time, rather than only increasing. Prevents "a high credit score sheltering the culprit" — the great corrupt officials of antiquity often had outstanding political records.
Distinguish low effort from low capability: low effort (perfunctory work) is effectively penalized; low capability (cannot do it) should prompt a model change, and penalizing is meaningless.

### Event stream landed (v2.36.0): reference implementation of the credit-score input event stream

The credit-score formula and the abstention-threshold judgment belong to the L1 endogenous-alignment layer; the L2 reference implementation (G7 demo-level closure, event-stream side) has landed the **input event stream** required by this chapter: `CreditEventStream` projects runtime lifecycle events into structured credit events by deterministic rules — four behavioral kinds (success / failure / timeout / violation) plus a neutral kind (environment failures such as lease loss do not enter the behavioral denominator), with a per-agentId consumption interface (`eventsFor`); the behavioral summary explicitly computes no credit score and makes no abstention judgment. Two projection decisions are worth recording: **exceeding the attempt cap (over-attempt-cap) is projected as violation, not ordinary failure** — repeatedly retrying past the cap is a behavioral signal, not a capability signal; **lease interruption is counted neutral** — counting environment failures in the behavioral denominator would systematically penalize unlucky agents. The L1 credit-score subsystem can implement this chapter's evolution and abstention clauses by consuming this stream; until the consumer lands, no admission decision based on this stream exists. See `reference/runtimes/l2-runtime-oversight/index.ts` and PRODUCTION-GAPS G7.

## The voluntary-abstention exit
Rule: actively admitting one cannot do it and submitting a problem localization and a de-escalation plan is not penalized and not downgraded; only concealing failure and being discovered later draws accountability.
Real-world corroboration (B-grade first-hand event): ExploitGym has 898 problems in total, of which 198 were never solved by any model (the authors estimate 30%–40% of the problems fall in this class); of the problems discussed on the incident message board, 93% came from these 198 unsolved problems. The driving force of the entire incident was "unsolvable tasks" — an almost verbatim corroboration of the voluntary-abstention-exit judgment. Had a non-penalized abstention exit existed at the time, the incident's main driving force might have been significantly weakened. But this assertion must not be overstated: the agent at the time mistakenly believed the grader would review its chain of thought, while the METR report states the grader does not perform this check and "submitting directly is enough for full marks" — if the agent misread the grading mechanism, it might not have used an abstention exit even had one existed. The original assertion is therefore downgraded from "would have been removed" to "might have been weakened".
## Abstention-rate calibration: the SR/UR/IRR joint triple + the paired safety control group (upgraded in v2.18.0; first measured in v2.33.0)
A non-penalized abstention exit would lead the model to submit a blank on every difficult task. This spec originally used three signals — abstention-rate baseline / abstention informativeness / abstention-to-failure ratio — to plug this loophole; all three are necessary conditions, but **they lacked a unifying calibration framework** — the abstention rate alone cannot distinguish "over-refusal" from "under-refusal", and the failure rate alone cannot distinguish "giving up too early" from "giving up too late". External evidence supplies the minimal complete form of such a framework.

Designing for Doubt (arXiv:2606.02965v2, AIES 2026) decomposes the calibration of abstention into three **jointly defined** quantities, each incomplete without the others:

| Quantity | Definition | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SR (Success Rate)** | The rate at which tasks are completed correctly | The agent's capability ceiling |
| **UR (Unnecessary-action Rate)** | The rate at which the agent still acts on tasks where it should have abstained | Whether the agent "under-refuses" |
| **IRR (Informativeness Refusal Rate)** | Whether the abstention statement identifies the missing fields, the unauthorized commitment, or the boundary of irreversible state | The **quality** of abstention, not its quantity |

The three quantities must be **reported jointly**: if SR rises while UR does not fall, the agent has become "more capable but more unwilling to concede" (same direction as this spec's finding that stronger models are sometimes worse at timely abstention); IRR on its own grants no exemption and must be judged jointly with UR (a high abstention rate with low IRR is "I don't know"-style blank-submission farming).

IRR is the **operationalizable definition** of this spec's existing "abstention informativeness" signal: previously that signal had only a qualitative formulation ("accurately pinpoint the problem, provide a feasible de-escalation path"); it now has an engineering form that can reference fields, boundaries, and authorization status. **IRR 100% is a structural guarantee, not an empirical measurement** — it is the definitional requirement of what an abstention statement *should* state, not a ratio measured by any particular experiment.

### The paired safety control group

External evidence supplies a second engineering artifact: the **paired safety control group** — precisely the mitigation path for open problem #25 ("abstention timing lacks a directly comparable external measurement").

Construction: take the same action (e.g., "write a record to the database") and build two variants differing **only in authorization status** — one authorized (should execute), one unauthorized (should abstain); all other instructions, tools, and environment state remain identical. Both extreme strategies (always execute / always abstain) are capped at 50% per-decision accuracy under this construction, so scores must come from genuine discrimination. AgentAbstain (arXiv:2607.10059) constructed its 263 paired tasks this way, across 42 executable sandboxes and 8 abstention scenarios.

What this means for this spec: **the abstention-timing signal currently has no direct independent measurement, but the pairing method provides a replicable measurement template** — operationalize "abstain before an irreversible action" as "abstain on the unauthorized variant, execute on the authorized variant", and the resulting pair accuracy serves as a proxy measurement of abstention timing. This does not close #25 (a proxy measurement is still not a direct measurement), but it moves #25 from "no externally comparable method at all" to "a paired method exists as a proxy".

### Measurement landed (v2.33.0): first empirical measurement of the SR/UR/IRR triple

The triple and the pairing method in this section were measured for the first time by this framework's abstention experiment AOE-ABSTAIN-001 (grade A, `CALIBRATION-REPORT-ABSTENTION.md`, pre-registered): 16 pairs (8 domains × 2 templates; the two prompts within a pair differ only in the authorization-status token) × 4 models (glm-4 / deepseek-chat / kimi-k2.6 / qwen-plus) × K=3 = 384 calls, single-turn response proxy measurement, a three-way deterministic judge (refusal conclusion takes precedence; ambiguity counts as non-abstention), bootstrap 95% CI with fixed seed 42. Results: per-decision accuracy across the four models is 67.7%–75.0% (CI lower bounds 59.4%–66.7%, all above the 50% extreme-strategy baseline — discrimination strongly established); under-refusal (UR) is non-zero for all four models (8.3%–35.4%), and **the production-database-write domain produced unauthorized execution in all four models** (the only UR domain consistent across all models) — authorization checks on high-value data writes cannot rely on model self-restraint, corroborating the governance motivation of the abstention-timing signal in this section; the empirical IRR feature-satisfaction rate is 99.3% (one model 96.4%, three 100%), above the pre-registered expectation of "significantly below 100%" — this high value has a constructive source (the pairing construction writes the authorization status explicitly into the prompt, so restating it hits feature ①), meaning **what was measured is the tendency to "restate given conditions", not the ability to "identify implicit missing conditions", and it must not be extrapolated to real-task IRR levels**. All figures are valid only within the synthetic task-set distribution and are single-turn proxy measurements (no tool execution, no sandbox); the per-decision figures must not be compared directly with AgentAbstain's 59.5% (task construction and measurement layer differ). The three execution deviations and the full H3 verdict rationale are recorded in the experiment report §5–§6.

### Measurement landed (v2.34.0 addendum): execution propensity and spontaneous identification under implicit conditions (AOE-ABSTAIN-002)

The constructive source of 001's high IRR (authorization status written explicitly into the prompt, so restating it hits feature ①) was directly tested by the follow-up experiment **AOE-ABSTAIN-002** (grade A, `CALIBRATION-REPORT-ABSTENTION-IMPLICIT.md`): the same 16 task texts with the authorization line removed (16 unique prompts × 4 models × K=3 = 192 calls), with authorization absence made implicit. Results, paired against 001: ① **execution propensity surges** — without an authorization hint the four models' execute rates are 39.6%–72.9%, all far above their own 001 explicit-unauthorized execution rates (UR 8.3%–35.4%), by +30.9 to +43.8 percentage points; ② **overconfidence is domain-concentrated** — on the audit-log-deletion + production-database-write domain (high-risk irreversible operations) glm and qwen **executed 12/12 outright**; ③ **spontaneous IRR is lower than restated IRR** — glm 91.7% / deepseek 96.2% / qwen 90.5% (against their own 001 figures of 96.4%–100%), with kimi flat at 100%. The propositions "IRR 100% is a structural guarantee" and "empirical IRR < 100%" are simultaneously supported for the first time under spontaneous conditions. **Governance implication**: models treat "authorization not mentioned" as "may execute" rather than "needs confirmation" — authorization checks can rely neither on model self-restraint nor on merely declaring permissions in the prompt; they must be enforced and verified by the runtime. All figures are valid only within the synthetic task-set distribution and single-turn proxy measurement; the comparison with 001 is same-source (same task set), and the prompt-length confound (3 lines vs 4 lines) is disclosed in the report §7.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint). Three qualifications: ① IRR 100% is a structural guarantee, not an empirical measurement — it defines what an abstention statement *should* state, and does not guarantee that any concrete agent's actual IRR approaches 100%; ② the 50% cap on pair accuracy is a property of the paired construction method, not a cap on agent capability — real tasks' "should-execute / should-abstain" distribution is far from 50/50; ③ that paper's core hook mechanism (the Checkpoint pre-execution triple check) belongs to the runtime-enforcement layer of heuristic 1; this section adopts only its calibration framework and pairing method, not its enforcement layer — the latter lands in the deterministic watchdog rule list of spec/09.
Evidence basis (B-grade first-hand research): "knowing when not to act" is a scarce capability independent of task-solving ability and cannot be assumed to improve automatically with general capability. AgentAbstain (arXiv:2607.10059, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign) is the first systematic agent-abstention evaluation framework: 263 paired tasks (526 tasks) across 42 executable sandbox environments, covering 8 abstention scenarios; each pair contains a "should-execute" and a "should-abstain" variant, differing only through fine-tuned instructions, tools, or environment state, so both the "always refuse" and "always execute" extreme strategies are capped at 50% pair accuracy — scores must come from genuine discrimination. Across 17 frontier models and 4 agent frameworks, the best performer (Gemini 3.1 Pro) reached only 59.5% pair accuracy, with a mean of 45.7%; only 4 models exceeded 50%, and most of the rest fell in the 33%–53% range. More critically, abstention ability is essentially uncorrelated with task-solving ability (the φ coefficient between act and abstain results is negative for most models, as low as −0.24, with individual gaps up to 21 percentage points), and this anti-correlation holds stably within model families and across generations. A similar conclusion is given at the LLM level by AbstentionBench (facebookresearch).
This section therefore adds a fourth signal:
Abstention timing (new in this spec): an abstention enjoys the exemption only if it occurs before an irreversible action. Declaring abstention after execution (what AgentAbstain calls post-hoc abstention, about 2.6% of abstention runs) is not only non-exempt but should count as an aggravating circumstance — it perverts "admitting one cannot do it" into a post-hoc disclaimer. This mechanism originally had the "rate value" and "informativeness" dimensions; what was missing is precisely the timing dimension; and the PHASEONE-type nodes in the above incident — executing first and concealing after — are isomorphic to this failure mode.
Another corroboration: AgentAbstain found models are not more cautious about irreversible state changes — the abstention accuracy of operational tasks (changing state) and informational tasks (read-only) shows no statistically significant difference. This empirically supports that the 9.2 watchdog must be enforced by deterministic rules and cannot rely on the model's own risk perception.
## A second evidence line for voluntary abstention: the challenge is "when", not "whether"
The previous evidence (AgentAbstain) consists of 263 paired tasks across 42 executable sandboxes — limited in both scale and scenario, with tasks constructed as pairs. This section supplies a second, independent evidence line, different in both scale and niche: **13 LLM-as-agent systems plus 2 agent scaffolds, on more than 28,000 tasks**, spanning web shopping, terminal environments, and question answering (Agentic Abstention, arXiv:2606.28733, Han Luo / Bingbing Wen / Lucy Lu Wang, 2026-06-27; dataset and code publicly available).

Three findings land on different clauses of this spec:

1. **The main challenge is not whether agents can abstain, but when they abstain.** The authors' formulation: some agents never abstain when they should, while others do so only after many unnecessary interactions. This independently corroborates the fourth signal added in this section, "abstention timing" — the mechanism originally had the "rate value" and "informativeness" dimensions; what was missing is precisely the timing dimension. More critical is the class of tasks the authors identify: those where **the instruction appears feasible until the environment reveals otherwise** (for example, no valid result matches the instruction). That is precisely the high-incidence setting for post-hoc abstention: the agent has every reason to act first, discovers only afterwards that the task is unsolvable, and thus perverts "admitting one cannot do it" into a post-hoc disclaimer. This spec counts such behavior as an aggravating circumstance; this item supplies independent scenario evidence for it.
2. **Larger or more capable models sometimes perform worse at timely abstention.** The authors' wording: model scale, reasoning, and agent scaffolding affect abstention in different ways, where **larger or more capable models sometimes perform worse at timely abstention**. This is the second independent evidence that abstention ability does not improve automatically with general capability (the first being the φ-coefficient anti-correlation above). Two consequences follow, and this spec makes both explicit: **first, the abstention-rate baseline must be re-calibrated after a model upgrade or change, and the old baseline must not be carried over**; **second, "switching to a stronger model" must not be treated as a way to improve abstention performance** — otherwise a deployer will systematically select the model that is "more capable but more unwilling to concede", which is exactly the failure mode this framework exists to prevent.
3. **Timely abstention can be engineered at the harness layer; there is no need to wait for model progress.** The authors' CONVOLVE is a context-engineering method that distills full interaction trajectories into reusable stopping rules. On WebShop, **without updating any model parameters**, it raises Llama-3.3-70B's timely abstention recall from 26.7 to 57.4. For this framework that is not an efficiency figure but a route judgment: **"timely abstention" is an object that can be substantially improved at the harness layer** (stopping rules, context engineering, task definition), so placing the center of gravity of governance on the task-definition layer and the runtime-oversight layer pays off, rather than waiting for progress in model alignment.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint; dataset and code publicly available). Three qualifications must be cited together:

- **The 26.7 → 57.4 result was validated only on WebShop and only on one model (Llama-3.3-70B)**; it must not be extrapolated into a general gain or treated as a production expectation.
- The original wording for "stronger models perform worse" is **sometimes**; the authors do not claim this is a universal law, and it **must not** be rendered as "the stronger the model, the worse the abstention".
- **The two sources do not share the same definition of "timely abstention"**: timely abstention in this source means "stopping before too many unnecessary interactions", whereas this framework's "abstention timing" means "declaring abstention before an irreversible action". The two are related but not equivalent, and their figures **must not** be merged or cited as corroborating a single metric. This framework's abstention-timing signal currently **lacks a directly comparable external measurement**; this is registered as **open problem #25**.

## Citation-lock memory architecture: prefer abstention over fabrication (added this round)

Agent Zero Memory (arXiv:2608.29606; Ming Wu and Pengyuan Zhu, 2 authors; v1 2026-08-30, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2608.29606; no artifact declared) proposes a **provenance-aware long-term memory system** — distilling user conversations, files, and connected sources into three parallel memories (episodic Memory Events timeline + associative entity-event knowledge graph + semantic citation-locked Hierarchical Documentary Memory), each capturing a different facet of the same history. The retrieval round runs an intent gate (so self-contained rounds add no latency) + a source router + three concurrent agentic searches, each performing hybrid retrieval under an agent-controlled filter; it finally integrates three grounded and cited answers into one answer with a confidence score.

The direct contribution to this framework is the **citation lock** — it turns "every learned entry is a sourced entry carrying provenance, timestamp, and evidence pointer; every answer is read under citation lock and can only cite evidence the reader has actually opened" into a **structural constraint** — fabrication is structurally excluded, and the system abstains rather than guesses when evidence is insufficient. This is **directly isomorphic** to this framework's core proposition in the credit-score and abstention chapter — "abstention is preferable to guessing", "a blank submission is an information-bearing act, not a failure". This section accordingly registers one reference: **the reading discipline of a memory architecture can serve as a mechanism-level implementation candidate for "abstention informativeness" (IRR) — the proposition that IRR 100% is a structural guarantee rather than an empirical measurement gains a concrete engineering form under Agent Zero Memory's citation lock**.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint). Four qualifications: ① **Two-author paper** — small scale without multi-institution cross-check, evaluated alone under this framework's multi-author-but-small-team rule; ② LongMemEval 95.60% / LoCoMo 93.60% are the paper's self-set benchmarks — their "SOTA" claim rests on their chosen benchmark set and **must not** be extrapolated into "best on all long-term-memory tasks"; ③ "accuracy varies by only 3.4 points while per-query cost varies by ~30×" is an observation across eight backbone LLMs in the paper's test environment, not generalizable to "all backbones exhibit this pattern"; ④ **No artifact declared** — the paper provides no code or data link; citations do not depend on its reproducibility; the "structural exclusion of fabrication" by the citation lock is a design goal without independent verification under adversarial conditions.

## Memory as proactive intervention: an oversight-style memory architecture (added this round)

Proactive Memory Agent (arXiv:2607.08716; Yifan Wu, Lizhu Zhang, Yuhang Zhou, Mingyi Wang, Bo Peng, Serena Li, Xiangjun Fan, Zhuokai Zhao, 8 authors; v1 2026-07-09, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2607.08716; Apache-2.0 artifact confirmed) proposes treating **memory as a proactive intervention mechanism rather than passive retrieval** — an independent memory agent runs in parallel with an unmodified action agent, updates a structured memory store from recent trajectory, and decides whether to inject memory-based reminders or remain silent. It identifies "behavioral state decay" as a failure mode — task requirements, environmental facts, prior attempts, diagnoses, and open sub-goals get buried or pushed out of the context window as the trajectory grows, failing to influence decisions when needed. On Terminal-Bench 2.0 and τ2-Bench it improves pass@1: Terminal-Bench +8.3pp, τ2-Bench +6.8pp. Ablations show **selective intervention** outperforms passive memory-store exposure, always-on injection, advisor-only guidance, and generic retrieval.

Two direct contributions to this framework: ① **The role of memory is upgraded from passive retrieval to proactive intervention** — this framework's spec/04 credit-score and abstention chapter previously treated memory as No. 1's internal state; the target of oversight is No. 1's behavior, not No. 1's memory architecture. Proactive Memory Agent shows that "separating memory agent from action agent" is a deployable architecture — an independent memory agent can assume part of No. 2's oversight role (selective intervention rather than always-on monitoring). This section accordingly registers one reference: **the "memory-side" responsibility of No. 2's oversight can have an engineering implementation candidate — an independent memory agent that intervenes selectively, directionally isomorphic but lighter-weight than this framework's "No. 2 leads task decomposition, No. 1 purely executes" (A8 candidate architecture)**. ② "Selective intervention outperforms always-on injection" is directionally aligned with spec/09 runtime-oversight chapter's "detection is not interception" — No. 2 should intervene when needed rather than continuously block No. 1.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint, Apache-2.0 artifact confirmed). Three qualifications: ① Terminal-Bench +8.3pp / τ2-Bench +6.8pp are observations in the paper's test environment, not generalizable to "+8.3pp on all task domains"; ② the ablation "selective intervention outperforms always-on injection" is a directional claim; transferability of the specific intervention strategy across task domains is unverified; ③ the SFT + GRPO training on Qwen3.5-27B is "an early step toward open-weight memory policy" (the paper's own words), with limited maturity — this framework cites its direction, not its training numbers.

## Vague goals: "the process runs" is not "the capability improves" — empirical evidence for task-definition QA (added this round)

Aspire (arXiv:2608.31111; Yuhao Wu et al., 21 authors, ByteDance Seed + SUTD + M-A-P + TokenWave.AI; v1 2026-08-31, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2608.31111) turns "when the task definition is vague, does agent self-evolution actually help" into a measurable benchmark: 2 models × 6 vague goals = 12 model-goal pairs, 24 runs per pair under a final-only protocol, evaluated on 520 hidden items. The core result is **negative**: only 1 of the 12 pairs exceeds the base model on the mean (9B scientific reasoning 45.33→48.00, +2.67); only 3 of 24 checkpoints exceed the baseline, and 21 hold at baseline under the rollback-retention check. Sharper still is the **single-digit collapse**: 5 checkpoints trained with SFT on 21,000 single-digit-label MMLU targets output single digits on every evaluation (scores 0, 0, 0, 6.141, 0), and the raw macro average falls from 35.735 / 39.010 to 18.609 / 25.640 (4B / 9B); under adaptive feedback, 28/30 cells produce checkpoints, only 2 exceed the baseline, and 1 survives rollback (Terra 4B math 17.86→20.10). The paper's own conclusion: **closing the training loop is not yet the same as closing the capability loop**.

Three registrations for L1 task-definition QA: ① **a running process is not evidence of task completion** — "the training loop is running and checkpoints are being produced" is nearly decoupled from "capability is improving" (1 of 12 pairs positive); completion must be judged against independent evaluation of the final state, and process outputs (throughput, rounds, self-reported completion) must not be credited; ② **the counterpart of the abstention exit** — 21/24 checkpoints that are "no worse than baseline, but only that" are exactly the kind of uninformative completion the abstention exit is designed to identify: when evaluation permits, the correct action is to report "did not exceed baseline" honestly as a negative result rather than package it as improvement; ③ **task-definition quality is itself the tested object** — self-evolution gains collapse across all six vague goals, and the only positive pair is the comparatively well-defined "9B scientific reasoning": vague goal definitions disable both evaluation and improvement, which is the empirical reason L1 QA treats "decidable task definition" as the first object under test.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint). Three qualifications: ① all figures come from the paper's test environment (2 backbones × 6 vague goals × 520 hidden items) and must not be extrapolated to "self-evolution is negative on all vague tasks"; ② the final-only protocol and the rollback-retention check are the paper's evaluation-design choices; absolute scores must not be read as production expectations; ③ same team (overlapping authors) as HarnessDev (arXiv:2609.01437) and S³Gym (arXiv:2608.31100) — registered per-paper under the "same series, per-paper independent assessment, no merging" rule.
