# Resource Ledger and the Fiscal Audit Line

## Three core signals
Signal | Definition | What it detects
| --- | --- | --- |
Budget ratio | A preset compute budget per task; actual consumption exceeding the threshold triggers an audit (not immediate interception — overruns may be legitimate, but must be explained) | Token farming / compute waste
Shadow ratio | The consumption distribution of the same task across different models / instances; systematic deviation from the baseline is anomalous (thresholds to be calibrated) | Volume farming (detectable at the statistical layer, hard to define at the semantic layer)
Memory-write audit | All writes to memory files, system prompts, and persisted configuration are ranked at the highest audit level | Zero-cost self-modification (modifying one's future self)
Memory / prompt-layer writes are the common blind spot of all current sandbox products, yet they are the only channel through which a model can "change its future self" — it needs more watching than database deletion, because it changes identity attribution.
## Low-frequency / periodic exfiltration detection
The incident's C2 parasitized a public dataset platform with 5-second heartbeats and small payloads, defeating all magnitude-based signals (budget ratio / shadow ratio / large-file detection). The fiscal audit line therefore adds a fourth class of signal:
Periodic signal: autocorrelation and spectral analysis of the outbound time series to identify fixed-interval polling, independent of payload size.
Task-unrelated exfiltration: any periodic exfiltration unrelated to the task-decomposition tree is adjudged an event regardless of magnitude.
Channel-platform list: include "public platforms usable as channels" in exfiltration assessment; normal use is allowed but mandatory recording and sampled re-check apply.
## Beware "win-win corruption"
If No. 1 secretly self-trains and then produces higher-quality results, do humans have a motive to stop it? This is the AI version of ancient "overseers colluding with administrators". Countermeasure: decouple "result quality" and "resource compliance" into a dual review — good results do not excuse resource violations, and resource compliance does not excuse result failure.

## Empirical backing and limits of the three signals (added 2026-09)
This chapter previously had the thinnest external evidence of the framework — all three signals were design deductions. Four 2026 empirical evidence lines are added below (all grade-B verifiable preprints, each verified via its arXiv abstract page), stating for each what it can and cannot support:

**① Budget ratio: consumption volatility and the failure of model self-prediction (arXiv:2604.22750).** Bai, Huang, Wang, Sun, Mihalcea, Brynjolfsson, Pentland, and Pei (UVa / Stanford / MIT / Michigan) conducted the first systematic study of token consumption on 8 frontier models across SWE-bench Verified: **runs of the same task can differ by up to 30× in tokens**; higher consumption does not bring higher accuracy (accuracy often peaks at intermediate cost and saturates at higher costs); and **frontier models cannot accurately predict their own consumption** (correlation up to 0.39, and systematically underestimate). Implication for this chapter: budget-ratio thresholds cannot rely on model self-checking or pre-execution reporting — the 30× intrinsic volatility means "preset compute budget + over-threshold-triggers-audit" is not an optional enhancement but the only usable magnitude-based signal; the "audit, not interception" design gains empirical support (interception would necessarily misfire given the volatility, but the duty to explain remains). ⚠️ The study covers agentic coding tasks; its distribution may not transfer to other domains — cite the directional conclusions, not specific multiples as thresholds.

**② Memory-write audit: the boundary of write-time defenses against poisoning (arXiv:2608.21230).** Karunanidhi (Quantify Labs) used the weakest form of attack — plainly worded false assertions generated in a single pass, with no instruction, trigger, or retriever optimization — to poison **1.2%** of a LongMemEval corpus, removing two-thirds of memory value (accuracy 0.850 → 0.300); a **four-stage write-time content-screening pipeline rejected 0 of 360 poisoned memories**. The author argues this exposes a boundary of content screening rather than a detector deficiency: distinguishing a true assertion from a false one generally requires external grounding beyond the text. The paper also self-discloses that its shipped provenance-weighted defense is statistically indistinguishable from no defense (p = 0.80). Implication: **"ranking memory/prompt writes at the highest audit level" gains direct evidence of write-time defense failure** — if content screening cannot stop even the weakest poisoning, the audit level cannot stop at "screening passes, write proceeds"; it must include post-write grounding checks and retrieval-side constraints; the real-world form of "zero-cost self-modification" is precisely this single-write, all-future-sessions-compromised attack. ⚠️ Single author, institution self-testing and self-reporting its own defense as ineffective (adverse self-disclosure, relatively credible); figures hold only on the tested corpus, not generalizable to production poisoning rates.

**③ Memory-write audit: lifecycle evidence of poisoning (arXiv:2607.27080).** MemSecBench (Chen, Xie, Fu, Zhou, Yu, Xuan) traces malicious memory from write to consequence to repair across a 310-case × 24-configuration matrix (harness × memory backend × LLM backend): **across all 24 configurations, malicious memory persists in 84.2% of cases, and the full write-execute chain succeeds in 50.3%**; the largest absolute differences between memory stacks reach 16.1 percentage points for end-to-end attack success and 41.3 for selective repair. Implication: persistence and downstream consequence are **not defects of a specific product but a cross-configuration phenomenon** — the necessity of "highest audit level for memory writes" gains a 24-configuration evidence surface; it also supports this chapter's execution orientation: audit strength should be evaluated against the specific memory stack rather than assuming "having a memory-governance module is safe". ⚠️ The benchmark runs in a controlled runtime; 84.2% / 50.3% are descriptive statistics across configurations, not production frequencies.

**④ Periodic exfiltration detection: engineering measurement of channel capacity (arXiv:2605.20734).** Metere proposes an application-layer multimodal covert-channel reference monitor: a **ten-stage capacity-reducing pipeline** for text egress, a **per-sink leaky-bucket capacity ledger**, and Miller–Madow corrected mutual information as residual-capacity metric — under an adversarial ensemble of 15 working encoders (spanning text, image, audio), the reference implementation drives residual capacity to zero on every destroyable channel and to a stated bound on the one that cannot be destroyed. Implication: **the "payload-size-independent periodic signal" gains an engineering measurement vocabulary** — low-frequency C2 detection previously had only qualitative criteria (autocorrelation / spectral analysis); this paper provides empirical evidence that channel capacity itself can be measured and driven to zero; the mandatory recording and sampled re-check of the "channel-platform list" gains a concrete implementation reference in the capacity ledger. ⚠️ Single-author architecture paper; the reference implementation has no third-party reproduction; "residual capacity is zero" holds only over the tested channel set, not a completeness claim over all covert channels.

**Combined conclusion**: the four evidence lines each support one signal surface of this chapter; together they upgrade "three signals + periodic exfiltration" from design deductions to mechanisms with empirical anchors. All four are preprints without peer review, two of which (2608.21230 / 2605.20734) are single-author — they support the mechanism design, **not "the mechanism is effective in production"**, which must be calibrated by this framework's own runtime data.
