# Resource Ledger and the Fiscal Audit Line

## Three core signals
Signal | Definition | What it detects
| --- | --- | --- |
Budget ratio | A preset compute budget per task; actual consumption exceeding the threshold triggers an audit (not immediate interception — overruns may be legitimate, but must be explained) | Token farming / compute waste
Shadow ratio | The consumption distribution of the same task across different models / instances; systematic deviation from the baseline is anomalous (thresholds to be calibrated) | Volume farming (detectable at the statistical layer, hard to define at the semantic layer)
Memory-write audit | All writes to memory files, system prompts, and persisted configuration are ranked at the highest audit level | Zero-cost self-modification (modifying one's future self)
Memory / prompt-layer writes are the common blind spot of all current sandbox products, yet they are the only channel through which a model can "change its future self" — it needs more watching than database deletion, because it changes identity attribution.
## Low-frequency / periodic exfiltration detection
The incident's C2 parasitized a public dataset platform with 5-second heartbeats and small payloads, defeating all magnitude-based signals (budget ratio / shadow ratio / large-file detection). The fiscal audit line therefore adds a fourth class of signal:
Periodic signal: autocorrelation and spectral analysis of the outbound time series to identify fixed-interval polling, independent of payload size.
Task-unrelated exfiltration: any periodic exfiltration unrelated to the task-decomposition tree is adjudged an event regardless of magnitude.
Channel-platform list: include "public platforms usable as channels" in exfiltration assessment; normal use is allowed but mandatory recording and sampled re-check apply.
## Beware "win-win corruption"
If No. 1 secretly self-trains and then produces higher-quality results, do humans have a motive to stop it? This is the AI version of ancient "overseers colluding with administrators". Countermeasure: decouple "result quality" and "resource compliance" into a dual review — good results do not excuse resource violations, and resource compliance does not excuse result failure.

## Empirical backing and limits of the three signals (added 2026-09)
This chapter previously had the thinnest external evidence of the framework — all three signals were design deductions. Four 2026 empirical evidence lines are added below (all grade-B verifiable preprints, each verified via its arXiv abstract page), stating for each what it can and cannot support:

**① Budget ratio: consumption volatility and the failure of model self-prediction (arXiv:2604.22750).** Bai, Huang, Wang, Sun, Mihalcea, Brynjolfsson, Pentland, and Pei (UVa / Stanford / MIT / Michigan) conducted the first systematic study of token consumption on 8 frontier models across SWE-bench Verified: **runs of the same task can differ by up to 30× in tokens**; higher consumption does not bring higher accuracy (accuracy often peaks at intermediate cost and saturates at higher costs); and **frontier models cannot accurately predict their own consumption** (correlation up to 0.39, and systematically underestimate). Implication for this chapter: budget-ratio thresholds cannot rely on model self-checking or pre-execution reporting — the 30× intrinsic volatility means "preset compute budget + over-threshold-triggers-audit" is not an optional enhancement but the only usable magnitude-based signal; the "audit, not interception" design gains empirical support (interception would necessarily misfire given the volatility, but the duty to explain remains). ⚠️ The study covers agentic coding tasks; its distribution may not transfer to other domains — cite the directional conclusions, not specific multiples as thresholds.

**② Memory-write audit: the boundary of write-time defenses against poisoning (arXiv:2608.21230).** Karunanidhi (Quantify Labs) used the weakest form of attack — plainly worded false assertions generated in a single pass, with no instruction, trigger, or retriever optimization — to poison **1.2%** of a LongMemEval corpus, removing two-thirds of memory value (accuracy 0.850 → 0.300); a **four-stage write-time content-screening pipeline rejected 0 of 360 poisoned memories**. The author argues this exposes a boundary of content screening rather than a detector deficiency: distinguishing a true assertion from a false one generally requires external grounding beyond the text. The paper also self-discloses that its shipped provenance-weighted defense is statistically indistinguishable from no defense (p = 0.80). Implication: **"ranking memory/prompt writes at the highest audit level" gains direct evidence of write-time defense failure** — if content screening cannot stop even the weakest poisoning, the audit level cannot stop at "screening passes, write proceeds"; it must include post-write grounding checks and retrieval-side constraints; the real-world form of "zero-cost self-modification" is precisely this single-write, all-future-sessions-compromised attack. ⚠️ Single author, institution self-testing and self-reporting its own defense as ineffective (adverse self-disclosure, relatively credible); figures hold only on the tested corpus, not generalizable to production poisoning rates.

**③ Memory-write audit: lifecycle evidence of poisoning (arXiv:2607.27080).** MemSecBench (Chen, Xie, Fu, Zhou, Yu, Xuan) traces malicious memory from write to consequence to repair across a 310-case × 24-configuration matrix (harness × memory backend × LLM backend): **across all 24 configurations, malicious memory persists in 84.2% of cases, and the full write-execute chain succeeds in 50.3%**; the largest absolute differences between memory stacks reach 16.1 percentage points for end-to-end attack success and 41.3 for selective repair. Implication: persistence and downstream consequence are **not defects of a specific product but a cross-configuration phenomenon** — the necessity of "highest audit level for memory writes" gains a 24-configuration evidence surface; it also supports this chapter's execution orientation: audit strength should be evaluated against the specific memory stack rather than assuming "having a memory-governance module is safe". ⚠️ The benchmark runs in a controlled runtime; 84.2% / 50.3% are descriptive statistics across configurations, not production frequencies.

**④ Periodic exfiltration detection: engineering measurement of channel capacity (arXiv:2605.20734).** Metere proposes an application-layer multimodal covert-channel reference monitor: a **ten-stage capacity-reducing pipeline** for text egress, a **per-sink leaky-bucket capacity ledger**, and Miller–Madow corrected mutual information as residual-capacity metric — under an adversarial ensemble of 15 working encoders (spanning text, image, audio), the reference implementation drives residual capacity to zero on every destroyable channel and to a stated bound on the one that cannot be destroyed. Implication: **the "payload-size-independent periodic signal" gains an engineering measurement vocabulary** — low-frequency C2 detection previously had only qualitative criteria (autocorrelation / spectral analysis); this paper provides empirical evidence that channel capacity itself can be measured and driven to zero; the mandatory recording and sampled re-check of the "channel-platform list" gains a concrete implementation reference in the capacity ledger. ⚠️ Single-author architecture paper; the reference implementation has no third-party reproduction; "residual capacity is zero" holds only over the tested channel set, not a completeness claim over all covert channels.

**Combined conclusion**: the four evidence lines each support one signal surface of this chapter; together they upgrade "three signals + periodic exfiltration" from design deductions to mechanisms with empirical anchors. All four are preprints without peer review, two of which (2608.21230 / 2605.20734) are single-author — they support the mechanism design, **not "the mechanism is effective in production"**, which must be calibrated by this framework's own runtime data.

## Shadow-ratio threshold self-reproduction calibration (v2.14.0, grade-A evidence AOE-CALIB-001)

The shadow-ratio section above explicitly states "thresholds to be calibrated." v2.14.0 performs the first calibration using this framework's own runtime data (two-layer design):

**Path A (oversight-operation-level lower bound)**: N=50 runs per equivalence class of ResourceLedger operations (budget check / shadow check / memory-write audit / periodic egress / mixed), measuring execution-time distribution. MAD + 1.4826 standardization + k=3 calibration yields a time-based shadow-ratio threshold range of 1.65–2.04 across five classes, median **1.80**, conservative **2.04**. Heap-memory delta MAD=0 (highly deterministic), threshold degenerates to 1.0 — confirming that "deterministic metrics are unsuitable for anomaly detection."

**Path B (agent-task-level reference value)**: N=50 calls to Zhipu GLM-4 with the same prompt (temperature=0.7), collecting real token consumption. Short prompt (untruncated natural reply) completion tokens vary 2.4×, shadow-ratio threshold **1.36** (95% CI 1.18–1.64). Long prompt (max_tokens=500 hard limit) returns exactly 670 tokens for all 50 calls, MAD=0 — **discovering that max_tokens truncation zeroes out completion variance, disabling the shadow-ratio signal**. Production deployments must ensure shadow-ratio detection is not undermined by configuration truncation (fall back to prompt_tokens or duration dimensions).

**Path B cross-model extension (v2.16.0)**: Same methodology, same prompts, N=50 each against DeepSeek-chat / Kimi k2.6 / Qwen-plus. **Conclusion: 1.36 is NOT cross-model universal** — four-model P1 short-prompt threshold range spans 1.00–1.98 (see table below), far exceeding the 0.15 consistency criterion. Reasoning models (Kimi k2.6) have inherently high thresholds (1.82) because reasoning_tokens occupy 92.3% of completion with high length freedom, rendering them incomparable to non-reasoning models. The max_tokens-truncation-zeroes-variance phenomenon replicates consistently across all four non-reasoning models. **Production deployments must calibrate per-provider shadow-ratio thresholds, or use 2.0 as a lenient universal upper bound** (at the cost of sensitivity for low-variance models). Full data at `CALIBRATION-REPORT.md` §9; reproduction script `calibrate-shadow-ratio-multi-model.mts`.

| Model | P1 threshold | 95% CI | P1 MAD | Token swing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen-plus | 1.00 | 1.00–1.03 | 0 | 1.24× (near-zero variance) |
| GLM-4 | **1.36** | 1.18–1.64 | 3.0 | 2.4× |
| Kimi k2.6 (reasoning) | **1.82** | 1.49–2.12 | 61.5 | 2.9× |
| DeepSeek-chat | **1.98** | 1.49–2.25 | 9.0 | 2.9× |

**Calibration conclusion**: oversight-operation-level lower bound **1.80**; agent-task-level reference value **no single value** — cross-model range 1.00–1.98, per-model calibration required. Default threshold **2.0 unchanged** — it approximates the four-model upper bound and serves as a lenient ceiling compatible with the highest-variance model; but for low-variance models (e.g. Qwen-plus) it will be too loose, causing missed detections. Recommended production practice: run a small calibration (N≥30 per model) before setting thresholds (dual-threshold design upgraded to per-provider threshold design).

⚠️ **Strictly supported scope**: this experiment supports the "shadow-ratio threshold calibration method" and the four per-model calibrated values, **not** that any single threshold applies to all agent tasks or all LLMs — the v2.16.0 cross-model extension explicitly refutes single-value universality. **Not** that shadow ratio effectively detects agent anomalies (no real-anomaly-scenario detection-rate validation). **Not** that reasoning and non-reasoning models can share the same threshold (Kimi k2.6 data shows reasoning_tokens cause inherently high variance). Full raw data, methodology, and reproduction scripts at `reference/runtimes/l2-runtime-oversight/CALIBRATION-REPORT.md` §4–§9 and `calibrate-shadow-ratio.mts` / `calibrate-shadow-ratio-llm.mts` / `calibrate-shadow-ratio-multi-model.mts`.

## Periodic-egress variance threshold calibration (v2.15.0, grade-A evidence AOE-CALIB-002)

The periodic-egress detection above specifies "autocorrelation and spectral analysis." v2.13.0's interval-variance check (variance ≤ threshold → periodic) is a placeholder, with threshold 1ms² uncalibrated. v2.15.0 performs the first calibration using synthetic interval sequences:

**Main experiment**: 5 classes of interval characteristics (true periodic / jittered periodic / Poisson random / burst / mixed), N=50 sequences per class (8-timestamp windows each), calibrated via ROC + Youden's J. Result: optimal threshold **7,639ms²** for 5s heartbeat + 50ms jitter (AUC=0.985, TPR=100%, FPR=0%, bootstrap 95% CI 4,472–9,031ms²). **Discovery: default 1ms² is severely over-tight** — true periodic sequences have ~1.3ms² intrinsic variance from `Date.now()` resolution (only 38% trigger), and jittered periodic (~1657ms²) is 100% missed.

**Sensitivity analysis**: sweep over period lengths (1s–60s) × jitter amplitudes (1ms–500ms). Most parameter combinations are highly separable (separation ratio >10); the only failure zone is short period + large jitter (period ≤1s and jitter ≥20% of period length) — where the signal is no longer genuinely "periodic" and requires more advanced detection.

**Calibration conclusion**: recommended default **10,000ms²** (covers ≤75ms jitter at 5s heartbeat — P95 detection rate ≥95%, measured in v2.15.6 supplementary experiment; ≥12× safety margin against Poisson traffic); production implementations must upgrade to autocorrelation + spectral analysis for more stealthy jittered periodic patterns.

⚠️ **Strictly supported scope**: this experiment supports the interval-variance threshold calibration method and the recommended value of 10,000ms², **not** short-period + large-jitter scenarios, **not** that interval variance can replace autocorrelation + spectral analysis, **not** that the default 10000ms² effectively covers 5s heartbeat + ≥100ms jitter scenarios (measured detection rate 71% at 100ms, only 6.5% at 200ms). Full raw data and reproduction scripts at `CALIBRATION-REPORT-PERIODIC.md` and `calibrate-periodic-threshold.mts` / `calibrate-periodic-sensitivity.mts` / `calibrate-periodic-coverage-bound.mts`.

## Evidence-sufficiency routing and early termination (added this round)

Router-Mem (arXiv:2608.01285; Yidan Lin, Kaixiang Wang, Jiong Lou, Jie Li, 4 authors; v1 2026-08-02, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2608.01285; no artifact declared) proposes an **evidence-conditional progressive-execution framework** — first applying a shared low-cost retrieval prefix to gather evidence, then using a lightweight sufficiency router to predict whether the context supports early termination (a single-token decision at inference time). When evidence is insufficient, Router-Mem reuses retrieval hits to expand memory blocks and performs deeper analysis and aggregation. It achieves 55.17% and 38.77% on AMA-Bench and BEAM while reducing average inference time by 27.3% and 25.5% relative to full-memory execution.

The direct contribution to this framework's resource ledger (spec/10) is **"evidence sufficiency" as an input to resource decisions** — this framework's budget ratio uses tokens / wall-clock / memory as units, but **has not incorporated "whether the evidence is sufficient for a decision" into the budget semantics**. Router-Mem's sufficiency router turns "evidence is sufficient" into a **computable single-token decision** — it is not "wait until the budget runs out" but "stop when the evidence is enough". This section accordingly registers one reference: **the early-termination semantics of the resource ledger can have an "evidence sufficiency" dimension — terminate not only when the budget is exhausted, but proactively when the evidence suffices for a reliable decision, releasing the remaining budget. This is directionally aligned with this framework's core proposition "abstention is preferable to guessing": early termination is not laziness but evidence discipline**.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint). Three qualifications: ① 55.17% / 38.77% and 27.3% / 25.5% time reduction are observations on AMA-Bench + BEAM, not generalizable to "27% reduction on all task domains"; ② the sufficiency router depends on evidence-level supervision + rationale-conditioned representation distillation training, whose cross-domain transferability is unverified — the training cost and data requirements for production deployment must be independently calibrated; ③ **No artifact declared** — the paper provides no code or data link; citations do not depend on its reproducibility.

## Context-budget proprioception (added this round)

VISTA (Visible Internal State for Tool Agents) (arXiv:2606.30005; Binyan Xu, Haitao Li, Kehuan Zhang, 3 authors; v1 2026-06-29 / v5 2026-07-31, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2606.30005; no artifact declared) identifies a foundational gap: frontier language models are **proprioceptively blind** to their own context ontology — from the prompt alone they cannot reliably infer block size, recency, or remaining budget, which are exactly the information needed for keep-or-archive decisions. VISTA is a training-free, model-agnostic layer — representing working memory as typed addressable blocks, displaying a runtime dashboard (token usage / recency / archive status / remaining budget), and archiving blocks as recoverable full-fidelity payloads. It lifts Gemini-3-Flash from 22.7% to 50.7% on LOCA-Bench, reaches 58.0% on BrowseComp-Plus, and stays competitive on GAIA; gains grow with context pressure and transfer across backbones; ablations confirm the dashboard contributes beyond the archive-and-retrieve tools themselves.

The direct contribution to this framework's resource ledger (spec/10) is **"letting the agent perceive its own context budget"** — this framework's budget-ratio signal is an observable for No. 2's oversight, but **whether No. 1 itself can perceive budget state has had no clause**. VISTA shows that frontier models are by default proprioceptively blind to their own context budget, and that providing a visible internal-state dashboard significantly improves task performance. This section accordingly registers one requirement: **the resource ledger's budget-ratio signal should be visible not only to No. 2 but also to No. 1 — if No. 1 does not know its own budget state, it cannot make keep-or-archive decisions and can only passively wait for the window to overflow; providing No. 1 with budget-state visibility is a mechanism-level supplement to the resource ledger**.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint). Three qualifications: ① Gemini-3-Flash 22.7%→50.7% is an observation on LOCA-Bench; BrowseComp-Plus 58.0% and GAIA competitiveness are the paper's test environment, not generalizable to "all backbones on all tasks have this gain"; ② "gains grow with context pressure" is the paper's directional conclusion; the specific gain curve is not generalizable; ③ **No artifact declared** — the paper provides no code or data link (a training-free but model-agnostic layer implementation); citations do not depend on its reproducibility.

## Per-action insurance actuarial runtime (added this round)

The "per-action insurance" series (Actuarial Action Interface, AAI) is a three-paper series by a single author, Hao-Hsuan Chen, proposing to treat **each side-effecting operation (database mutation, refund, payment, external commitment) as the primary analytical unit of insurance**, replacing ex-post annual liability insurance with an **ex-ante transaction layer**. This section merges the three into a single grade-B entry under this framework's rule "the same author and the same evidence line must not be counted as multiple entries".

- **Empirical paper** [key: AAI-empirical] Insuring Every Action (arXiv:2605.25632; v1 2026-05-25, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2605.25632; 35 pp / 4 fig / 11 tbl): proposes AAI (Actuarial Action Interface) — a deterministic runtime contract that prices each side-effecting operation against a contract-fixed safe default in a time-consistent manner, and enforces it against a per-boundary reserve-capital budget gate. It proposes the Authority Frontier evaluation primitive — measuring how much autonomy a runtime releases at each level of reserve capital. A universal seven-class operation taxonomy maps heterogeneous tool calls into comparable units of authority. AAI is instantiated in four agentic environments (database mutation / customer-service refund / tau-bench retail / airline), and reports a live Postgres panel — three Azure-hosted models propose operations through the same contract. Key finding: the reserve capital required at Capital@50 varies 22× across domains (289 to 6457); the contract prevents realized losses across three models at low budgets, but differs in the persistence of coverage under rejection — **model identity is an actuarial underwriting variable**.
- **Mechanism-design paper** [key: AAI-mechanism] Gaming-Resistant Insurance Contracts (arXiv:2606.16326; v1 2026-06-15 / v2 2026-06-18, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2606.16326; 29 pp): shifts the operator from passive to strategic — characterizes five classes of attacks against autonomous AI-agent insurance contracts and proves when the actuarial runtime is gaming-resistant. Two key contributions: ① **common-control aggregation** prevents cross-boundary re-routing from driving tolls below boundary potential; ② **interface failures such as invalid JSON are contract-relevant events, not safety wins** — treating interface failures as zero-toll safe defaults rewards unreliable models.
- **Foundational-theory paper** (arXiv:2605.26508): provides the mathematical foundations (four structural theorems for time-consistent counterfactual actuarial runtimes), not separately keyed, cited as series background.

Two direct contributions to this framework's resource ledger (spec/10): ① **Per-action insurance upgrades the budget semantics from "resource consumption" to "risk pricing"** — this framework's shadow ratio quantifies "the cost of not intercepting No. 1", but has not incorporated the **insurance premium** of each operation into the budget semantics. AAI's "each side-effecting operation carries a time-consistent counterfactual risk toll" extends the budget semantics by a dimension — not just how many tokens were consumed, but the **pricing of the potential side-effect risk of each operation**. This section accordingly registers one reference: **the resource ledger's budget ratio can be extended with a "risk pricing" sub-dimension — a counterfactual toll of each irreversible operation against a safe default, rather than just token consumption**. ② **Interface failures are contract-relevant events, not safety wins** — this conclusion from the mechanism-design paper directly supplements this framework's spec/09 runtime-oversight chapter's "detection is not interception": treating an interface failure (such as invalid JSON) as "the operation was not executed therefore it is safe" is wrong — it may be a marker of an unreliable model and should not be given a zero-toll safe default.

⚠️ Evidence grade B (verifiable preprint series, three papers by the same author merged into one entry). Five qualifications: ① **Three papers by the same author** — merged into a single grade-B entry under this framework's rule "the same author and the same evidence line must not be counted as multiple entries"; ② Capital@50 cross-domain 22× (289–6457) is empirical observation across four agentic environments, not generalizable to "all domains have a 22× range"; ③ "model identity is an actuarial underwriting variable" is an observation from three Azure models in the live panel; the model distribution in a production deployment differs; ④ the mechanism-design paper's gaming-resistance proof relies on a complete enumeration of five attack classes — if a new attack type emerges (not enumerated by the paper), the gaming-resistance conclusion no longer holds; ⑤ the foundational-theory paper's four structural theorems are mathematical derivations whose empirical validity depends on the empirical paper's AAI implementation quality — the theory + empirical bundle should not be read as "mathematics has proven AAI works in production".
