# Sources of Enforcement Power: Who Pays, and Why

This is the key chapter of the framework. The entire framework assumes a rational, prudent principal willing to pay; but oversight costs are immediate, certain, and quantifiable, while the risks it prevents are distant, probabilistic, and unquantifiable — this is the adverse-selection problem the insurance industry has not solved for millennia.
Anchor | Mechanism | What is lost by not paying
| --- | --- | --- |
Industry admission | Make oversight compliance a precondition for participating in specific businesses / platforms | Losing bid and access qualification
Regulatory compliance | Fold into industry regulatory requirements; incident disclosure and evidence chains become compliance obligations | Facing penalties and suspension
Insurance premiums | Trade oversight compliance for premium discounts on cybersecurity / AI liability insurance | Premiums rise or coverage is refused
Client audit | Require evidence chains and third-party verification reports in major-client procurement | Losing major-client orders
If this chapter could not be written, all twelve preceding chapters would be mere design drawings. The ancient oversight systems operated not because their documents were well written, but because of the violent backing of imperial power.
Policy and legal basis (new in this spec): the four anchors above are not pure deduction; real institutional counterparts already exist. ① Legal hierarchy: the amended Cybersecurity Law (passed at the 18th session of the Standing Committee of the 14th National People's Congress on 2025-10-28, effective 2026-01-01) adds Article 20, which explicitly requires "improving AI ethics norms, strengthening risk monitoring, assessment, and security regulation" and encourages "using new technologies such as AI to improve cybersecurity protection" — the "regulatory compliance" anchor thus gains a legal-hierarchy basis above departmental rules. ② Institutional model: the Measures for the Identification of AI-Generated Synthetic Content (effective 2025-09-01) and its accompanying mandatory national standard "Cybersecurity Technology — Methods for Identifying AI-Generated Synthetic Content" establish a dual "explicit + implicit" labeling system; implicit labels embed generation/synthesis content attribute information, service-provider names, or codes via metadata, "providing technical assurance for content tracing and liability determination", and open the full chain of "design-development — listing-distribution — user-use — content dissemination — liability tracing"; this is highly isomorphic at the content layer to this framework's E subsystem (evidence externalization) and No. 3-B (hash-chain notarization), serving as a real-world engineering model. ③ The Interim Measures for the Management of Generative AI Services (effective 2023-08) establish security assessment and algorithm filing, training-data disclosure, and classified/graduated regulation; as of 2025-03, more than 300 generative-AI products and more than 3,000 generation/synthesis services had been filed. ④ Industry practice: sector regulators such as telecommunications have issued AI business security-protection schemes (compute operations and maintenance, log auditing, data classification and grading, algorithm filing and pre-launch security assessment); the "industry admission" anchor is not without a source.
